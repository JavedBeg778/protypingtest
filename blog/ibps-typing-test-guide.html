IBPS Exam Typing & Descriptive Test Guide 2026: PO, Clerk & Banking Requirements

📅 Updated: November 2026 ⏱️ 10 min read ✍️ By Javed Beg 🎯 Fact-Checked Banking Syllabus
⚠️ Crucial Exam Clarification:

There is widespread confusion among banking aspirants regarding typing tests. IBPS PO and IBPS Clerk do NOT have a standalone speed qualification test (such as 30 or 35 WPM transcription tests). Instead, IBPS PO Mains includes an online Descriptive Paper (Letter & Essay) conducted directly on a computer keyboard. IBPS Clerk does not have any typing or descriptive exam, but relies on a state-level Local Language Proficiency Test (LPT). Read below for precise, verified syllabus details for each banking post.

2. The IBPS PO & SBI PO Descriptive Test: Format & Interface Quirks

In the IBPS PO Mains Examination, the Descriptive English Paper commences immediately after the objective sections conclude. Understanding the technical setup prevents unexpected score deductions on exam day:

  • Structure: Candidates must answer two questions:
    • Letter Writing (Formal or Informal): Typically 150 words. Topics range from banking grievances and loan inquiries to letters to editors on financial topics.
    • Essay Writing: Typically 250 words. Topics focus on economic reforms, digital banking, central bank policies, ESG investing, or contemporary social issues.
  • Time Limit: Exactly 30 minutes for both questions combined (total 400 words).
  • Total Marks: 25 marks (IBPS PO) / 50 marks (SBI PO). Evaluation is computerized and qualifying in nature (scores contribute to final merit in SBI, and meet cutoffs in IBPS).
  • Interface Restrictions (TCS iON Environment):
    • Clipboard Disabled: Keyboard shortcuts like Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, and Ctrl+X are strictly locked. You cannot copy-paste text.
    • Backspace Functionality: Backspace is enabled, but extensive backspacing burns valuable composing time.
    • Word Counter: A live word counter sits below each text area. Exceeding or falling below the prescribed limit (typically ±10% tolerance) triggers penalty deductions.
    • Membrane Keyboards: Exam centers frequently use standard, well-worn membrane keyboards with deep key travel and varying tactile resistance.

3. The Speed Math: Why 30 WPM is the Real Benchmark

Aspirants often ask: "If I only need to type 400 words in 30 minutes, isn't that just 13.3 WPM?"

This mathematical assumption fails in practice because typing an exam response is creative composition, not straight transcription from an existing sheet:

Effective Time Breakdown (30-Minute Window):
• Topic Selection & Brainstorming Outline: 5 Minutes
• Actual Keyboard Input Time: 18 Minutes (400 words)
• Proofreading, Fixing Typos & Punctuation: 7 Minutes

Required Composing Speed = 400 words / 18 minutes ≈ 22.2 Net WPM
Required Raw Transcription Speed = 30 to 35 Gross WPM

When typing while formulating sentences, cognitive load slows your finger throughput by 30% to 40%. A candidate who types at only 18–20 WPM on transcription tests will consistently run out of time during the essay, leading to incomplete conclusions and lost marks.

4. Structured 4-Week Banking Typing & Writing Schedule

Here is a tested preparation plan tailored specifically for banking aspirants balancing descriptive writing with quant and reasoning prep:

Week 1: Touch Typing Fundamentals & Ergonomics

  • Focus: Eliminate the hunt-and-peck habit. Memorize home row, upper row, and bottom row finger assignments.
  • Daily Drill: 20 minutes on Touch Typing Lessons. Do not look down at the keyboard.
  • Benchmark: Achieve 20+ WPM with 95% accuracy on plain lowercase English paragraphs.

Week 2: Punctuation, Capitalization & Banking Numbers

  • Focus: Banking writing requires formal punctuation (commas, semicolons, hyphens) and frequent capital letters (RBI, KYC, NPA, IFSC, NEFT).
  • Daily Drill: 15 minutes of capitalization drills using both Left and Right Shift keys properly.
  • Practice: Practice with Tricky Keys Practice to strengthen pinky finger control for apostrophes and quotes.

Week 3: Financial Vocabulary & Speed Building

  • Focus: Typing multi-syllabic financial terms fluidly without hesitating on spelling.
  • Daily Drill: Complete two sessions on our 5-Minute Typing Test targeting sustained cadence.
  • Benchmark: 30+ Gross WPM with 96%+ accuracy on economic prose.

Week 4: Timed Full Simulation (30-Minute Real Mocks)

  • Focus: Open a bare notepad or text editor with spellcheck disabled. Set a 30-minute timer.
  • Exercise: Spend 10 minutes typing a 150-word formal letter to a Bank Manager. Spend 17 minutes typing a 250-word essay on a current banking topic. Use the final 3 minutes strictly for proofreading.
  • Review: Check for recurring spelling errors, run-on sentences, and paragraph formatting.

6. Common Pitfalls in Banking Keyboard Tests

  1. Over-relying on Auto-Correct: Smartphone keyboards make us forget tricky spellings like accommodation, bureaucracy, sovereignty, and surveillance. TCS iON has zero autocorrect.
  2. Violating Paragraph Structure: When writing your essay, ensure a 3-part layout: Introduction (40 words), Body Paragraphs (160 words), and Conclusion (50 words). Pressing Enter twice between paragraphs creates clear visual demarcation.
  3. Ignoring the Formal Letter Skeleton: A formal letter scores marks on structure: Sender address, Date, Receiver designation, Salutation, Subject Line, Body, and Formal Sign-off. Ensure you practice typing addresses and subjects without typo errors.
  4. Neglecting Keyboard Hygiene: In testing centers, keyboards can be dusty or have sticky Spacebars. Always use the 5-minute pre-test system test to verify that every single alphanumeric key, the Spacebar, and the Backspace key register cleanly on your screen. Request an invigilator for an immediate replacement if any key sticks.

7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is any computer typing certificate required to apply for IBPS PO or Clerk?

No. IBPS notifications do not ask for a certified typing certificate. They mandate basic computer literacy (such as having studied computers in high school/college or possessing an introductory computer operations certificate). Self-issued skill certificates from online typing sites do not satisfy government qualification clauses.

What happens if I type 220 words instead of 250 in the essay?

Most bank evaluation schemes allow a ±10% tolerance (e.g., 225 to 275 words for a 250-word prompt). If your word count falls significantly below 225, you receive minor mark deductions for incomplete coverage. Writing under 150 words generally results in severe score penalties.

Does EPFO SSA use the same pattern as IBPS PO?

No. EPFO SSA (conducted by NTA) is a strict 10-minute speed transcription test: you must type a printed passage on screen at 35 WPM (English) or 30 WPM (Hindi). Unlike IBPS, you do not write essays; your speed and mistake percentage are the deciding criteria.

Can I use backspace in the IBPS descriptive paper?

Yes, backspace is completely functional. However, mouse cut-and-paste and keyboard shortcuts (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+Z) are disabled. Rely on continuous clean typing rather than rewriting large paragraphs.
